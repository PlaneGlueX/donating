# Combat Rifle remake: design spec (tools\guns\m16.js)

**Target.** PG3D's current Combat Rifle (the wiki's "Appearance", Combatriflenew.png) at the Photon Shotgun's level of detail. It's a chunky M16A2. The metal is navy steel, with pale blue-steel inset plates. It has:
- a big box compensator with an H-shaped inset;
- a thick barrel with a chrome strip;
- a tall A front sight;
- a massive ribbed handguard with a central bar and a framed front plate;
- a box scope with a cyan lens on a carry-handle mount;
- a receiver with a light window frame and an L-shaped plate carrying one red selector;
- a navy magazine with three light ribs;
- a short, tall box stock with an inset window.

**Recommendation (the realistic option).** Build the current look, and drop the bayonet and the snow camo. The bayonet comes from the old "Old Combat Rifle" skin, and the snow camo from the classic look.
- The navy and pale-steel palette already keeps the gun apart from the black-and-red AK-48 in the hotbar, which was the camo's only job.
- Keep our overall length (butt 27.6, muzzle face -5.8), the grip, the magazine, the scope (sight line and rearX), the T-handle and every frame pivot.
- Recover the reference's front-heavy, short-stock look inside those limits:
  - split the stock into a low neck and a short, tall block;
  - make the handguard, barrel and muzzle much chunkier;
  - move the front sight back to the handguard's front end.

**Cautious alternative.** Keep today's identity on the new silhouette: snow-camo blotches on the pale plates, and the bayonet re-hung under the new muzzle box. That costs about 5.8 units of length again and needs a new bayonet mount. The owner's open question in PLAYTEST 180 about the colors still applies.

## 1. What the reference looks like, part by part

### How the numbers were measured

The icon is a 3/4 render:
- the barrel axis runs 34° up and right;
- the gun's vertical leans 15° (the magazine's ribs are the check: they come out exactly vertical);
- the camera sits a little in front of the gun and above it, so the front end faces and the top faces are visible.

To get a side profile:
- I unsheared the icon along those two axes;
- I scaled it so the stock's side-face rear edge to the muzzle box's side-face front edge is our 33.4 units: 0.1232 units per icon pixel;
- I took y from the bore at 7.8, at 0.113 units per pixel. That is about 8% less than x, because the muzzle's yaw toward the camera foreshortens x.

Error is about ±0.5 in x and ±10% in y. Silhouette tops include about 0.3 of visible top face.

We see the gun's **right side (-z)**. Our gui icon shows -z too (rotation [0,180,-30]), so the gui matches the icon. First person sees **+z**. PG3D mirrors its panels, so every panel below goes on **both sides** unless it says otherwise.

### The overall finding

PG3D's rifle is front-heavy:
- the stock is short and tall: 3.9 long, 4.6 tall;
- the action sits far back: the grip is 19% of the length from the butt (ours 27%), the magazine 29% (ours 44%);
- the handguard is the dominant block: 10 long and 6 tall.

The original and UP1 icons have the same proportions, so this is PG3D's M16 style, not a perspective artefact.

### Keeping the grip and the sights, and what moving them would break

The reference puts the action about 4.5 units further back, compacted by about 20% (scope 15.9..22.4, grip 21.3..23.3, magazine 17.0..19.2 in reference units). Matching that would break or force re-tuning of:
- **The ADS:** `aim({ rearX: 17.8 })` and the client check of the amber dot (PLAYTEST 180).
- **Frame pivots:** `SHOULDER` [18.4, 5.6, 8] (each round's kick) and `POSE_PIVOT` [14, 7, 8] (the magazine, handle and assist reload poses).
- **The grip's rotation origin.**
- **The T-handle's 2.6-unit pull and the handle pose.** The draw's and the reload's handle poses were tuned so the T-handle comes into view. M4A1.yml's sound delays (charge at tick 16 of the draw, 35 of the reload) are tied to those frames.
- **Display transforms:** the hip framing (firstperson [-5.26, 3.6, -1.79] at 0.64) and the third-person hand.

All of that was checked in the owner's client, so keep it.

The stock can't get shorter either. The pulled T-bar reaches x 23.2 at y 8.75..9.35, so anything taller than y 8.7 must start at x ≥ 23.4. That is why the stock is a neck plus a block.

## 4. What our model gets wrong or lacks

1. **Proportions.**
   - Our stock is long, slim and sloping (an A2 stock). The reference's is short, tall and boxy.
   - Our handguard is half the reference's height, and our barrel is half its thickness.
   - We have a small birdcage where the reference has a big box compensator.
   - Our front sight is a low post at the muzzle instead of a tall A at the handguard.
   - Our scope walls are thin.
2. **Palette.** Our metal is neutral grey-black (#1c1f27, #323844, #484e5c). The reference's is navy steel: blue shadows and pale blue-white highlights.
   - Our furniture is snow camo. The reference has navy furniture with pale steel inset plates.
   - Our scope has cyan side stripes. The reference has a pale top-edge bevel.
   - Our selector is grey, not the reference's single red accent.
   - Our magazine is grey with a black band, not navy with light ribs.
3. **The bayonet** belongs to the old skin, not the current look. M4A1.yml's lore says "A scoped battle rifle with a bayonet.", which needs a new line if the bayonet goes. CLAUDE.md's description too.
4. **Texture.** Our faces are one flat colour with a 1-texel ink outline at 2 texels a unit. There are no bands, bevels, panel seams, inset windows, ribs, slots or rivets. The reference uses every one of these.
5. **Glow.** Only the lens glows. The reference is the same, but the owner asked for glowing parts, so add one: the fiber.
6. **gui.** The icon was centred with the bayonet included. Without it, re-centre: on the blockout, `gui: { rotation [0,180,-30], translation [1.5, 1.8, 0], scale 0.5 }` fits the slot (`blk-full-gui-recentred.png`). Re-check `ground` and `fixed` (centre shifted +2.9 in x).
